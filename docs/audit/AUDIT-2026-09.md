# ppg-js audit, September 2026

Scope: the whole repository at commit `aa35109` (v0.2.0), read without taking
any comment, README claim, or test name as true. Every finding below that
carries a number was reproduced with a script in `docs/audit/scripts/`, run
against the real source via `node --import tsx`. Nothing in `src/` was changed.

The one-paragraph verdict: the capture layer is unusually well instrumented and
the quality gate is honest, but the biometrics on top of it are not yet
trustworthy enough for an open-source library other people integrate. The
heart-rate number is fine. The HRV numbers (RMSSD, SDNN, and everything the demo
derives from them) are dominated by measurement noise that the pipeline itself
creates, the frequency-domain cross-check is far coarser than it looks, and the
demo's autonomic "stress" indices are numerically wrong. Every real recording in
the repo comes from a single iPhone, so "cross-platform" is currently an
aspiration, not a tested property. All of this is fixable, and the fixes are
listed in priority order at the end.

## 1. What was checked and how

- Read every file under `src/`, `tools/`, `test/`, `examples/app/`, the build
  and CI config, and all Markdown.
- Ran `npm test` (11 suites, all pass) and `npm run build` (succeeds; note it
  dirties two tracked HTML files every time, see D5).
- Ran `tsc --noEmit` on the project's own tsconfig: 20 errors with `strict:
  false`, 58 with `--strict`. Nothing in CI catches this.
- Profiled all four real fixtures (`docs/audit/scripts/fixture-stats.mjs`).
- Measured the bandpass filter's true frequency response and the FFT
  heart-rate quantization (`docs/audit/scripts/numerics.mjs`).
- Drove the real `PPGMonitor` frame loop, the same way `test/live-parity.test.js`
  does, over synthetic signals with known ground truth at 24, 30, and 60 fps,
  with and without 10% dropped frames (`docs/audit/scripts/live-rmssd-floor.mjs`).
- Checked the demo's HRV formulas against their cited sources.

## 2. What is genuinely solid

Credit where it is due, because the rest of this document is critical.

- **Always-on raw recorder plus offline replay.** Recording every frame's
  channel means and replaying through the same code is the single best
  decision in the repo. It is why this audit could be quantitative.
- **The quality contract.** `quality.good` with a single `reason` string, and
  HR/RMSSD/SDNN forced to zero when it is false, is the right shape. Most
  camera-PPG projects show a number and hope.
- **Continuous peak stream across windows.** Computing IBIs over the whole
  peak history instead of per window, and warming the filter with the previous
  window, removes a real class of boundary artifacts. The commit history shows
  these were found from field logs, which is how it should be.
- **iOS camera-session resilience.** Track ended/muted handling, torch
  re-apply with a physical brightness check, lens-swap detection, and
  re-acquiring the same device are all things that only exist because someone
  watched the phone fail. Keep all of it.
- **Zero runtime dependencies, 37 KB minified.** Correct for a library.

## 3. Findings

Severity scale: **Critical** = produces wrong biometrics or blocks use on a
class of devices. **High** = materially degrades accuracy or reliability.
**Medium** = correctness or robustness issue with a bounded effect. **Low** =
hygiene.

### A. Signal processing and biometrics correctness

#### A1. Critical. The FFT cross-check discards the newest 15% of every window and has 14 bpm resolution at 60 fps

`src/core/fft.ts:56` copies `Math.min(n, fftSize)` samples: with the default
`windowLength: 300` and `fftSize: 256`, the last 44 samples of every window
(the most recent ones) are silently dropped, not zero-padded. There is no
window function, so spectral leakage is at its worst. And because the frequency
axis is `sampleRate / 256`, the bin width in bpm is `sampleRate * 60 / 256`:

| Camera rate | Window duration | FFT bin width | 64, 68, 72, 76 bpm all read as |
|---|---|---|---|
| 24 fps | 12.5 s | 5.6 bpm | 61.9 / 67.5 / 73.1 / 78.8 |
| 30 fps | 10.0 s | 7.0 bpm | 63.3 / 70.3 / 70.3 / 77.3 |
| 60 fps | 5.0 s | 14.1 bpm | 70.3 / 70.3 / 70.3 / 70.3 |

Every fixture in the repo was recorded at 60 fps, so on the developer's own
phone the "independent" FFT estimate can only ever say 56, 70, 84 or 98 bpm.
Three things consume this coarse number:

1. `crossCheckHeartRate` compares IBI HR against it with a 25% tolerance. At
   70 bpm that tolerance is 17.5 bpm and the quantization alone eats up to
   7 bpm of it, so the gate is far looser than the code implies.
2. The peak detector's refractory period is `0.6 * 60 / heartRateFFT`. A
   14 bpm error in the prior shifts the refractory by up to 20%.
3. When IBI HR is unavailable, `heartRate = heartRateFFT` is what the user
   sees (`heartRateRaw`).

Fix: use all 300 samples, apply a Hann window, zero-pad to 2048 or 4096, and
refine the peak bin with parabolic interpolation. That is a few lines and
brings resolution to about 1 bpm at any camera rate. Also check for
harmonic lock (see A6).

#### A2. High. The "0.75 to 4 Hz bandpass" is a single low-Q biquad that is already 6 dB down at its own band edges

`bandpassCoeffs` builds one RBJ constant-peak-gain biquad with
`Q = f0 / bandwidth = 1.73 / 3.25 = 0.53`. Measured magnitude after the
forward-backward pass (`numerics.mjs`):

| Frequency | Physiological meaning | Gain after filtfilt |
|---|---|---|
| 0.30 Hz | breathing (18 breaths/min) baseline wander | −20 dB |
| 0.50 Hz | 30 bpm | −12 dB |
| 0.75 Hz | 45 bpm, nominal low edge | −6 dB |
| 1.00 Hz | 60 bpm | −3 dB |
| 1.73 Hz | 104 bpm, the actual centre | 0 dB |
| 4.00 Hz | 240 bpm, nominal high edge | −6 to −7 dB |

Consequences: a resting athlete at 48 bpm has their fundamental attenuated
5 dB relative to its own second harmonic at 96 bpm, which pushes both the FFT
(A1) and the amplitude-threshold peak picker toward double counting. Meanwhile
respiratory baseline wander, which in fingertip camera PPG is routinely
several times the pulse amplitude, is only attenuated 20 dB. The filter shape
is not what the constants and README promise.

Fix: replace with a real 4th-order Butterworth bandpass (a 2nd-order
high-pass at about 0.5 Hz cascaded with a 2nd-order low-pass at about 5 Hz,
designed by bilinear transform), still zero-phase. Same cost, flat passband,
steep skirts.

#### A3. Critical. Beat times come from sample index divided by mean rate, not from frame timestamps, so RMSSD measures frame jitter

The live path stores per-frame timestamps (`frameTimestamps`) but only uses
them to compute one mean rate per window. Peak times are
`refinedIndex / sampleRate`. Any frame that arrives late or is dropped shifts
every subsequent beat in that window by a whole frame period, and the
difference-of-differences that RMSSD computes amplifies exactly that.

Measured on the real live loop with a synthetic 70 bpm signal whose true
beat-to-beat variability is known (`live-rmssd-floor.mjs`, 120 s, AC 3 counts):

| fps | dropped frames | true RMSSD | live RMSSD | live accepted beats / ~140 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 24 | 0% | 0.0 ms | 25.0 ms | 96 |
| 30 | 0% | 0.0 ms | 20.7 ms | 107 |
| 60 | 0% | 0.0 ms | 25.8 ms | 121 |
| 24 | 10% | 0.0 ms | **52.6 ms** | 90 |
| 30 | 10% | 0.0 ms | **52.9 ms** | 109 |
| 60 | 10% | 0.0 ms | **54.3 ms** | 116 |
| 30 | 0% | 22.8 ms | 40.0 ms | 113 |
| 30 | 10% | 22.8 ms | 63.9 ms | 109 |

A healthy resting RMSSD is roughly 20 to 60 ms. The pipeline's own noise floor
on a signal with zero variability is 20 to 26 ms, and it more than doubles
with 10% dropped frames. The 200 s iPhone fixture has 822 inter-frame gaps
longer than 1.6 times the median out of 11,127 frames, about 7%, so the
dropped-frame row is the realistic one, not the clean one. `test/sim.test.js`
already noticed this and widened its RMSSD tolerance to ±60 ms with a comment
calling it "a signal/detector-noise reality". It is partly that, but the
replay path (which resamples onto a uniform time grid before filtering)
reports 29 to 31 ms under the same 10% drops, so most of the excess is the
live path ignoring its own timestamps.

Fix, in order of leverage:

1. Resample each window onto a uniform grid from the recorded timestamps
   (`resampleUniform` already exists and is what replay uses), then map the
   refined peak index back through the grid to an absolute time. This alone
   closes most of the live/replay gap and makes the two paths one path.
2. Interpolate to a higher rate (100 to 250 Hz, cubic) before peak picking.
   The HRV Task Force recommends at least 250 Hz for RR timing; camera PPG at
   30 fps needs interpolation to get anywhere near beat timing of a few ms.
   This is standard in the smartphone-PPG literature (Peng et al. 2015).
3. Use a more stable fiducial than the broad systolic maximum: the maximum of
   the first derivative (steepest upslope) or the pulse onset via intersecting
   tangents. Pulse-onset timing has been shown to track ECG RR intervals better
   than peak timing (Posada-Quintero et al. 2013).
4. Report the noise floor. A library that says "RMSSD 34 ms ± 22 ms
   measurement floor at this frame rate" is more useful than one that says
   "34 ms".

#### A4. High. `windowLength` is in frames, so window duration, latency, FFT resolution and the settle logic all change per device

`windowLength: 300` is 5 s at 60 fps (the iPhone in the fixtures), 10 s at
30 fps (most Android phones under torch), and 12.5 s at 24 fps (low light or
thermal throttling). Everything downstream inherits this: time to first
number after settling, the "beats in the last 60 s" gate's effective cadence,
the FFT bin width (A1), the `frameTimestamps` scan, the ring-buffer reset in
`computeFrame`, and the live/replay parity test, which only holds because both
fixtures happen to be 60 fps (replay always uses a fixed 5 s window at 30 Hz).
The README's "fired once per window (~5s)" is only true on that one phone.

Fix: specify the window in seconds and derive the frame count from the
measured rate, or better, run the engine on a uniform resampled grid (A3)
where the window length is fixed by construction.

#### A5. High. The "AC/DC ratio" that gates on "Weak pulse" is raw peak-to-peak over the whole window, so motion passes the gate and pulse can fail it

`PPGMonitor.computeFrame` computes `peakToPeak(redChrono) / redDc` on the raw
channel over 5 to 12 s. That range is dominated by whatever moves most in the
window: baseline wander, a finger shift, exposure hunting. A motion burst
*raises* this ratio, and a perfectly clean but slow-drifting pulse can have a
huge ratio while the cardiac component is tiny. The same value picks the
channel (`selectChannel`), feeds the state machine's `acdcStreakOk`, drives
`qualityScore`, and is what the README calls "rejects a weak pulse".
`perfusionIndex` (std of detrended signal / DC) is closer but still not
cardiac-specific.

Fix: compute pulsatile amplitude from the bandpassed signal, for example the
median per-beat peak-to-trough amplitude between detected peaks, or the
cardiac-band power from the (fixed) FFT divided by DC. Keep the raw range as
a separate motion indicator.

#### A6. High. No harmonic or sub-harmonic guard anywhere

If the dominant FFT bin lands on the second harmonic (common with a strong
dicrotic wave, and made more likely by A1 and A2), the refractory period
shrinks to 0.3 times the true IBI, the detector accepts dicrotic notches as
beats, IBIs alternate short/long, `artifactRatio` climbs, and the user is told
"Irregular beats detected, hold still" for a purely algorithmic reason. The
reverse (FFT on a sub-harmonic at low heart rates after A2's attenuation)
halves the refractory prior in the other direction. `crossCheckHeartRate`
cannot help because it only asks whether two numbers agree, not whether one
is twice the other.

Fix: after finding the FFT peak, compare power at f/2 and 2f; if either is
within a few dB and physiologically plausible, prefer the lower harmonic
whose sub-multiple is not present. Also feed the cross-check a
harmonic-aware comparison (agree if ratio is near 1, flag specifically if
near 2 or 0.5).

#### A7. High. The session tachogram, and therefore the demo's whole HRV report, includes beats from windows the quality gate rejected

`PPGMonitor.computeFrame` pushes every entry of `ibiDetails` into
`this.tachogram` for every MEASURING window regardless of `quality.good`.
`examples/app/app.js:166` then feeds `getTachogram().filter(p => p.valid)`
into `HRVAnalysis.analyzeHRV`, whose header comment says it "uses only
accepted ('good window') beats — caller is responsible for that filter". No
one applies that filter. A window flagged "Irregular beats" or "Weak pulse"
still contributes its individually-valid IBIs to RMSSD, LF/HF, DFA and the
stress index. The empty-state copy ("No good-quality windows were captured")
is also untrue: it triggers on zero valid IBIs, not zero good windows.

Fix: tag each tachogram entry with the window's `good` flag (or the window
index) and have `getSessionSummary`/the report use only good-window beats, or
provide `getTachogram({ goodOnly: true })`.

#### A8. Medium. The SETTLING reset does not start a fresh window at slot 0 and leaves stale timestamps in the ring

`this.nFrame = DURATION` (100) with the comment "next tick starts a fresh
window at slot 0". The slot is `nFrame % windowLength` computed *before* the
increment at the end of `computeFrame`, so the reset frame writes slot 100 and
the next window boundary at `nFrame = 300` contains 99 slots of the fill value
(0.5 in `acdc`) followed by 200 real samples, a manufactured step exactly like
the one the `toChronological` comment warns about. `frameTimestamps` is not
reset at all, so `measuredSampleRate()` for that window spans stale samples
from before the reset and under-reports the rate. In practice the window is
usually still SETTLING so HR is not shown, but the under-reported rate feeds
the AC/DC computation and the state machine's own streak logic. The
`liveWindows` test helper also starts at `nFrame = 101`, so this path is
"tested" in its buggy form.

Fix: reset `nFrame` so the next write lands on slot 0, zero the timestamp
ring, and add a test that asserts the first post-reset window contains no
fill values.

#### A9. Medium. Two clocks and a rate-derived window start make cross-boundary IBIs drift

`sessionSec` comes from `Date.now()`; frame timestamps come from
`expectedDisplayTime` (performance.now domain). `windowStartSec = nowSec -
windowLength / sampleRate` uses the measured mean rate, so consecutive windows
can overlap or leave gaps in absolute time when the rate fluctuates. A 1.5%
rate change between two 10 s windows is a 150 ms error on the one IBI that
straddles the boundary, which is then either rejected as a jump or, worse,
accepted into RMSSD. Also `Date.now()` can step (NTP, timezone) mid-session;
`performance.now()` cannot. Fixing A3 (absolute timestamps per sample)
removes this whole class.

### B. Camera capture and platform behavior

#### B1. Critical on Android. All capture locks are bundled into one `advanced` constraint set, which the spec applies all-or-nothing

`_openCamera` builds a single object `{ torch: true, exposureMode: 'manual',
whiteBalanceMode: 'manual', focusMode: 'manual' }` and calls
`applyConstraints({ advanced: [advanced] })`. Per the Media Capture and
Streams constrainable pattern, each `ConstraintSet` in `advanced` is applied
only if *all* of its members can be satisfied together; otherwise that set is
skipped silently, without rejecting the promise. On a phone where, say, manual
focus is advertised but cannot be applied alongside torch (this happens on
several Android camera HALs), the torch request is dropped too, and the code
records `constraintsApplied: true`. This may be a hidden cause of "torch on
per settings, dark in practice" reports on non-iPhone devices.

Fix: `advanced: [{ torch: true }, { exposureMode: 'manual' }, { whiteBalanceMode:
'manual' }, { focusMode: 'manual' }]`, or separate calls, and read back
`getSettings()` per key to record what actually took effect.

#### B2. High. Exposure and white balance are locked before the finger is on the lens, and there is no saturation check

The locks are applied inside `_openCamera`, while the camera is still looking
at the room. Manual exposure mode without an explicit `exposureTime` freezes
whatever auto-exposure had converged to for the scene, which is the wrong
exposure for a torch-lit fingertip: on many Android sensors the red channel
then clips at 255, and a clipped channel has no pulse. Nothing measures the
fraction of clipped pixels; the only hint is a coaching string for
`redMean > 250`. Conversely, on iPhone the fixtures show `exposureMode` is
not exposed at all, so auto-exposure keeps hunting and white balance (which
*is* lockable there, and gets locked before the finger arrives) is frozen to
room lighting.

Fix: apply locks at the SETTLING to MEASURING transition, after the finger
is present and the auto algorithms have converged on it. Add a
`clippedFraction` per channel to the per-frame stats and to the quality gate,
and coach "less pressure / move off the flash" when it exceeds a few percent.

#### B3. Critical for cross-device use. Finger presence and drift use absolute 8-bit thresholds tuned on one iPhone

`isFingerPresent` is `red > 120 && green < 60 && blue < 60`. Drift gates are
6 and 12 counts. `MIN_CHANNEL_DC` is 40. The fixtures show why these work on
the iPhone 16-class device they came from: green sits at 0 to 14, blue at 9
to 25, red at about 190. Other devices do not look like that:

- Many Android ISPs under torch render a fingertip orange-pink with green in
  the 60 to 130 range. Presence never becomes true; the user sees "Cover the
  lens" forever.
- Auto white balance, which cannot be locked on iOS rear cameras and is
  locked *at the wrong time* elsewhere (B2), pushes green and blue gain up
  over the first seconds under a red cast. A finger that was "present" can
  become "absent" with no movement.
- Darker skin, thicker fingertips, a torch dimmed by thermal management, or
  a torch-less device lit by ambient light all lower red DC below 120.
- On a torch-less device the signal is reflectance from ambient light and the
  green channel is usually the better one, but the presence gate requires
  green to be dark.

Fix: make presence relative and adaptive. Chromaticity (red share of R+G+B
above about 0.55) plus a minimum red DC, plus a "pulsatile energy present"
term once the first window exists, is far more portable than absolute counts.
Expose all thresholds as options, record them in the debug log meta, and
collect fixtures from Android devices (see E and section 4).

#### B4. High. No screen wake lock

A three-minute protocol on an iPhone with the default 30 s auto-lock will not
finish. When the screen locks, the camera track ends and the resume logic in
this repo dutifully re-acquires it, but the measurement is gone. The demo
never requests `navigator.wakeLock`. It is supported on iOS Safari 16.4+ and
Android Chrome; request it in `start()` behind an option (default on for the
demo), re-request on `visibilitychange`, and release in `stop()`.

#### B5. High. The frame loop has no error boundary and no dropped-frame accounting

`computeFrame` re-arms the next callback at its end. Any exception before
that point (`drawImage` on a video whose `videoWidth` is momentarily 0 during
a resume, a `getImageData` security error, a null `roiSourceRect`) kills the
loop with no `onError` and no state change; the app just stops updating.
Wrap the body, report through `onError`, and re-arm.

Separately, `requestVideoFrameCallback` skips frames when the main thread is
busy, and the metadata carries `presentedFrames`, a monotonically increasing
counter that makes skipped frames countable. Log the deltas: the 7% gap rate
in the fixtures is either camera drops or main-thread stalls, and today there
is no way to tell. The metadata also carries `captureTime` for local camera
tracks in Chromium; prefer it over `expectedDisplayTime` when present.

#### B6. Medium. No capability pre-check and no error taxonomy

`navigator.mediaDevices.getUserMedia` is dereferenced unconditionally. In an
insecure context, an iOS in-app browser (Instagram, Facebook, many WebViews),
or an old browser, that is a `TypeError: undefined is not an object`, which
the demo shows verbatim in an alert. `getUserMedia` failures
(`NotAllowedError`, `NotFoundError`, `NotReadableError`, `OverconstrainedError`,
`SecurityError`) are all passed through raw. A library should map these to a
small stable set of error codes with user-facing guidance, and check
`isSecureContext` and `mediaDevices` before starting.

#### B7. Medium. 2x zoom is forced whenever the device advertises it

On several Android phones a zoom of 2 triggers a switch to the telephoto
module, which has no torch, different geometry, and often no close focus.
The finger covers the wide lens and flash; the telephoto sees the side of a
finger. Make zoom opt-in, or apply it only after confirming the device ID
and torch state are unchanged afterwards.

#### B8. Medium. One coaching branch hides every other message on any device without a torch

`coachingMessage` returns "Turn on your flashlight from Control Center" first,
before checking state. On any device where `getCapabilities().torch` is
falsy (desktop, front camera, many Android tablets, some Android phones) that
string is shown even while MEASURING with a good signal, because the good
branch is the only place `coachingMessage` is called. Reproduced: state
MEASURING, AC/DC 2%, torch unsupported gives the flashlight message.

#### B9. Medium. The README browser table is wrong about iOS

It says iOS Safari has no torch. All four fixtures are iOS 26.5 with
`trackCapabilities.torch: true`, `torch` applied, and `chosenLabel: "Back
Camera"`. WebKit has shipped the torch constraint for some time; the Safari
18.4 release notes even fix `getSettings()` returning stale torch and
white-balance values. The table should say torch works on iOS 17-class and
newer WebKit (Safari and every iOS browser), that `exposureMode` is not
exposed on iPhone rear cameras, and that `whiteBalanceMode` is.

#### B10. Medium. Calling `start()` twice on one instance leaks the previous session into the new debug log

`DebugRecorder.start()` resets `meta`, `lastT` and the anomaly counters but
not `samples`, `events` or `windows`. Reproduced: two samples, `start()`
again, one sample, `toJSON()` returns three samples with timestamps
1000, 1033, 500. Replay of such a file hits the non-monotonic path. `PPGMonitor`
likewise does not reset `tachogram`, `sessionWindows`, `frameCount`,
`_lastTrackSettings` or `displayedHeartRate` on restart. The demo avoids it by
constructing a new monitor per session; integrators will not know to.

#### B11. Medium. The library writes raw biometric data and the user agent to `localStorage` unconditionally

`_persistLastSessionLog` runs on every `stop()` and `pagehide`, storing up to
4.5 MB of per-frame PPG samples plus `userAgent` and screen size under a
fixed key on the integrator's origin. That is a feature for the demo's
"share last log" menu and a liability for anyone else: a PPG waveform is
health data, and an integrator may have promised users it never leaves
memory. Make persistence an explicit option (`debug.persistLastSession`,
default off), let the app turn it on, and strip `userAgent` by default.

#### B12. Low. Two-pass `getUserMedia` with no retry

Opening the camera, stopping it, and immediately re-opening by device ID is
a known source of `NotReadableError` on Android Chrome when the HAL has not
released the first session. A single retry with a short delay costs nothing.

### C. The HRV analysis module in the demo (`examples/app/hrv-analysis.js`)

This file is the "separate biometrics" part of the project today. It is not
in the npm package, has zero tests, and contains formula errors.

#### C1. Critical. The stress index formula is inverted

Baevsky's stress index is `SI = AMo / (2 · Mo · MxDMn)` with AMo in percent,
Mo and MxDMn in seconds. Kubios then reports the square root of that. The code
computes `1 / (2 · Mo · AMo · MxDMn)` with AMo as a fraction: AMo is in the
denominator instead of the numerator, and the scale is off by 100 on top.
Reproduced with Mo 0.9 s, AMo 40%, range 0.3 s: canonical 74.1, Kubios-style
8.6, this code 4.5. Because the SNS index is one third z-scored stress index,
and the z-score reference (`mean 100, sd 50`) matches neither convention, the
SNS index shown to users is not meaningful.

#### C2. High. The RR-to-4 Hz resampler is a sample-and-hold, not linear interpolation

`resampleRRTo4Hz` advances `j` until `beatTimes[j + 1] >= ti`, then
interpolates between `beatTimes[j - 1]` and `beatTimes[j]`, an interval that
ends *before* `ti`. The fraction is always above 1 and gets clamped, so the
output is the previous RR value held flat. Reproduced: a linear ramp of 20 RR
values resamples to 79 points with only 19 distinct values in plateaus. A
staircase adds broadband power to the spectrum that LF/HF then reports.

#### C3. High. The frequency-domain time axis is built from accepted IBIs only, so every rejected beat collapses a gap

`totalDurationSec` is the sum of accepted IBIs, and `resampleRRTo4Hz` stacks
them end to end. Two accepted beats 50 s apart become 1.6 s apart. The
tachogram carries real timestamps; use them, and treat gaps as missing data
(interpolate across short gaps, split segments across long ones).

#### C4. Medium. "TINN" is the histogram range

The code returns `(lastNonzeroBin − firstNonzeroBin + 1) · binWidth`, which is
simply max minus min quantised to 7.8 ms. Real TINN is the base of the
best-fit triangle to the histogram; the two diverge completely with a single
outlier (reproduced: one 300 ms outlier gives "TINN" 305 ms). Either implement
the triangle fit or label the field "RR range".

#### C5. Medium. VLF power is reported from 64 s Welch segments

With 256-sample segments at 4 Hz, the frequency resolution is 0.0156 Hz, so
the VLF band (0.0033 to 0.04 Hz) spans about two bins and is mostly the
detrending residual. The Task Force considers VLF uninterpretable below five
minutes. Hide it for short recordings, or report LF and HF only.

#### C6. Low. Small statistical inconsistencies

`std` uses the population estimator (divide by n) while `src/core/hrv.ts`
`sdnn` uses the sample estimator (n − 1), so the live SDNN and the report SDNN
disagree by design. `sampleEntropy` counts templates over `n − m` for length m
and `n − m − 1` for length m + 1; the standard definition uses `n − m` for
both. `correctArtifacts` uses a 20% median deviation rule, which is not the
Kubios threshold family it is described as.

#### C7. Medium. The PNS and SNS indices use invented reference distributions

`sd1nu` and `sd2nu` norms are commented as "documented estimate"; the stress
index norm is wrong (C1). Presenting a three-metric z-score average as
"vs. a normal population" is a claim the inputs cannot support. Either source
each reference (mean and SD, recording length, population) from a paper and
cite it per metric, or present these as relative trends against the user's
own baseline rather than population scores.

### D. Public API, packaging, documentation

#### D1. High. `package.json` points bundlers at an IIFE, and CJS consumers get no types

`"browser": "dist/index.global.js"` tells any resolver that honours the
`browser` field (webpack 4, some older tooling, several React Native setups)
to import a file that defines a global and exports nothing; `import PPG from
'@sontakey/ppg-js'` becomes `undefined` there. The `exports.require`
condition has no `types`, so TypeScript projects under `node16`/`nodenext`
resolution that `require()` the package are pointed at `index.d.ts` (ESM
syntax) and fail with the "masquerading as ESM" error even though
`index.d.cts` is built. tsup also warns that the CJS and IIFE entries mix
default and named exports, so `PPG.default` is what `require()` returns for
the default.

Fix: drop `browser` (or point it at the ESM build), add
`"require": { "types": "./dist/index.d.cts", "default": "./dist/index.cjs" }`,
and pick one default-export story.

#### D2. High. The types do not describe the API

`PPGMonitor`, `SignalProcessor`, `FingerStateMachine` and `DebugRecorder` are
all `[key: string]: any`. `PPGMetrics` declares ten of the ~25 fields callers
use (`quality`, `heartRateRaw`, `ibiDetails`, `artifactRatio`, `sampleRate`,
`settleRemainingSec` are all `unknown`). `PPGEventMap` declares a
`respiration` event that nothing emits and types `quality` as `unknown`.
`PPGOptions` advertises `source` and `video` as reserved and ignores them. A
TypeScript user gets red squiggles or `as any` on their first line.

#### D3. High. The DSP and HRV building blocks are not exported

`index.ts` exports `PPG`, `PPGMonitor`, `SignalProcessor`, `detrend` and the
FFT helpers. Not exported: `detectPeaks`, `computeIBIs`, `heartRateFromIBIs`,
`rmssd`, `sdnn`, `filtfiltBandpass`, `resampleUniform`, `evaluateQuality`,
`FingerStateMachine`, `coachingMessage`. Someone with a Polar strap's RR
intervals, an Apple Watch export, or their own camera capture cannot use any
of the math without going through the camera class. The `hrv` and `sources`
subpaths are empty modules. For a project whose stated goal is "a separate
library to extract biometrics", this is the gap.

#### D4. Medium. The README and other docs describe a different library

- The quick-start example does `import PPGMonitor from '@sontakey/ppg-js'`
  and passes `'#container'`; the default export is `PPG`, which is
  headless-only and ignores the container. `PPGMonitor` with a container
  renders nothing either: `uiRenderer` is hard-coded `null`, so every
  `ui.*` option in the documented defaults is dead.
- File paths cite `src/utils/*.js`, which do not exist.
- "onQualityUpdate fired once per window (~5s)" is only true at 60 fps (A4).
- The Roadmap says a TypeScript rewrite and removal of d3/fft.js are planned;
  both are done.
- The hero image is a placeholder; the Testing section lists nine suites,
  there are eleven; two fixtures are mentioned, there are four; "Acknowledgements"
  cites one paper for a pipeline built on several.
- `LIBRARY_SUMMARY.md` describes Rollup, D3, `UIRenderer.js` and a
  `ppg-monitor.css` that no longer exist. `rollup.config.js` is dead.
  `src/*.backup` files are committed.
- `CONTRIBUTING.md` says "rollup -> dist/" and names the author differently
  from `package.json`.
- `CHANGELOG.md` lists 1.0.0 (2024) below 0.2.0 (2026); semver readers will
  assume a regression.

#### D5. Medium. The build mutates tracked files, and the deploy serves the app shell as immutable

`tools/postbuild.mjs` rewrites `examples/app/index.html` and
`examples/demo/index.html` on every `npm run build`, so every build leaves
the tree dirty (observed after running it here). Meanwhile `vercel.json`
serves every `*.js` with `Cache-Control: public, max-age=31536000, immutable`,
but only `ppg.global.js` carries a version query; `app.js` and
`hrv-analysis.js` do not. A phone that has loaded the demo once will keep the
old `app.js` and `hrv-analysis.js` for up to a year while receiving new HTML
and a new core bundle. Version every script tag or move the cache-bust into
the Vercel build step.

#### D6. Medium. No type check or lint in CI, and the tests run through `tsx`

`tsc --noEmit` on the shipped `tsconfig.json` reports 20 errors today
(`Biquad` has no declared fields; `createDefaultOptions` reads properties off
`{}`). `tsup`'s declaration build tolerates them, so `dist/index.d.ts` is
produced from code the compiler rejects. CI runs only `npm test`. Add `tsc
--noEmit` and a formatter/linter to `test.yml`, and turn `strict` on file by
file (58 errors is an afternoon).

#### D7. Medium. Live and replay are two implementations by design

`SignalProcessor` runs on native frames at the measured mean rate;
`tools/replay.js` resamples to 30 Hz and re-implements the per-window loop
(channel selection, refractory, gating) in about 150 lines of copied logic.
`test/live-parity.test.js` exists precisely because they can drift, and it
only checks two 60 fps iPhone recordings with tolerances of 30% window
disagreement and 8 bpm. On a 30 fps Android phone the live path would use
10 s windows and replay 5 s windows; the test would not notice because there
is no such fixture. See section 4 for the structural fix.

### E. Testing and validation gaps

- **Every real recording is one phone.** All four fixtures: iPhone, iOS
  26.5.2, 480x640 at 60 fps, torch on, "Back Camera", `exposureMode`
  unavailable. There is no Android, no desktop webcam, no torch-less, no
  30 fps, no low-light, no darker-skin, no cold-hands recording. The
  `camera.test.js` Android case tests a regex on a label string.
- **No reference instrument, ever.** No session has been recorded alongside
  an ECG, chest strap or pulse oximeter, so the accepted-beat RMSSD has never
  been compared to anything except the pipeline's own synthetic generator.
- **The synthetic generator is kinder than reality in some ways and harsher
  in others.** It quantises to integers after spatial averaging (real
  fixtures are 100% fractional, so real resolution is better), but it never
  models auto-white-balance drift, saturation, a dicrotic notch, dropped or
  duplicated frames (the fixtures have both: `minDt` 1 to 4 ms means the
  same frame was delivered twice), or a 30 fps camera through the *live*
  path.
- **Tolerances were widened to pass.** `sim.test.js` documents moving the
  RMSSD bound from ±10 to ±60 ms; `live-parity` accepts 30% good/bad
  disagreement. Both comments are honest, and both are symptoms of A3.
- **No browser runs in CI.** The `getUserMedia` path, canvas readback, rVFC
  timing and the camera-resume flow are exercised only by hand-written stubs.
- **`hrv-analysis.js` has no tests at all**, which is how C1 and C2 survived.

## 4. How to make it actually work across phones

These are the structural moves. Individual fixes are listed under each
finding above.

**1. One streaming engine, timestamp-native.** Create `PpgEngine` with a
single method `push({ t, r, g, b })` that returns window results. Inside:
resample onto a uniform grid using the real timestamps (A3, A9), a fixed
window in seconds (A4), the corrected filter (A2), the corrected FFT (A1,
A6), a cardiac-band amplitude measure (A5), and the existing state machine
and gate. `PPGMonitor` becomes a thin camera adapter that pushes frames;
`tools/replay.js` becomes `for (const s of samples) engine.push(s)`. Parity is
then a property of the design, not a test with 30% slack.

**2. A device matrix and a fixture campaign.** The recorder and anonymiser
already exist; what is missing is the data. Minimum useful matrix: Pixel
(Google ISP), Samsung Galaxy (One UI camera HAL, known lens switching),
a mid-range MediaTek or Xiaomi device, an older iPhone (iOS 16/17), an iPad,
a desktop webcam with a desk lamp. For each: torch on and off, 30 and 60 fps
if selectable, a finger lift, a deliberate hand tremor, and, for at least two
subjects with different skin tones, a 60 s side-by-side with a chest strap.
Record what `getCapabilities()` returns; those objects are the ground truth
for B1 to B3 and belong in the repo as fixtures even without samples.

**3. Adaptive, logged thresholds.** Replace the absolute presence and drift
constants with relative ones (B3), expose them as options, and write the
effective values into `meta`. Every field bug report then carries the
thresholds that were in force.

**4. Lock exposure late, verify what took effect, check clipping.** Apply
`exposureMode`/`whiteBalanceMode` locks at SETTLING to MEASURING, one
constraint per `advanced` entry (B1), read `getSettings()` back per key, and
add `clippedFraction` to the per-frame stats and the gate (B2).

**5. A real browser in CI.** Chromium (already installed in this
environment) accepts `--use-fake-device-for-media-stream
--use-file-for-fake-video-capture=<file.y4m>`. Render each fixture's per-frame
RGB means into a flat-colour Y4M at the fixture's frame timing and run the
actual `getUserMedia` to canvas to `PPGMonitor` path under Playwright; assert
the same window results as replay. WebKit under Playwright covers the rVFC
timestamp path. This catches the class of bug that produced the 2026-09-12
"cold filter" regression before a phone does.

**6. Reference validation without a lab.** Web Bluetooth (Android Chrome,
desktop Chrome) exposes the Heart Rate Service, and Polar H10/H9 and
Garmin HRM straps stream RR intervals through it. A `BleHeartRateSource` (the
roadmap already names it) that records strap RR alongside camera samples in
the same debug log turns every developer session into a validation session,
and gives the `sources/` subpath its first real member. Publish Bland-Altman
plots for RMSSD and mean RR in the README instead of the current "not
validated" paragraph.

**7. Lifecycle and error hygiene.** Wake lock (B4), an error boundary in the
frame loop (B5), a capability pre-check and stable error codes (B6), opt-in
persistence (B11), full reset on restart (B10), and `presentedFrames`
accounting so dropped frames are counted rather than inferred.

## 5. Turning this into a biometrics library others can build on

The stated goal is a separate, reusable biometrics layer. Today the reusable
math is either unexported (D3) or living untested in the demo (section C).
Suggested shape, all under the existing package with subpath exports:

- `@sontakey/ppg-js/dsp`: pure functions on numeric arrays. `resampleUniform`,
  `butterworthBandpass` (zero-phase), `detectPeaks` (with a choice of
  fiducial), `computeIBIs`, `spectrumHR` (windowed, padded, harmonic-aware),
  `beatTemplateSQI`, `clippedFraction`. No DOM, no state.
- `@sontakey/ppg-js/hrv`: IBI array in, metrics out. Port `hrv-analysis.js`
  with C1 to C7 fixed and tests against published reference values (e.g. the
  PhysioNet RR series that Kubios and `hrv-analysis` Python packages use as
  regression sets). Time domain, frequency domain with proper gap handling,
  Poincaré, SampEn, DFA α1, plus the ultra-short-term variants noted in
  section 6. Every function states its minimum recording length and returns
  `null` with a reason below it.
- `@sontakey/ppg-js/engine`: the streaming `PpgEngine` from section 4, source-
  agnostic. Input is `{ t, channels }`; it does not know what a camera is.
- `@sontakey/ppg-js/sources`: `CameraSource` (today's `PPGMonitor` capture
  code), `ArraySource`/`FileSource` (replay), `BleHeartRateSource` (RR
  intervals straight into `/hrv`, bypassing `/dsp`).
- `@sontakey/ppg-js` (root): the `PPG` facade wiring `CameraSource` to
  `PpgEngine` with typed events, which is roughly what exists now.

Two naming points that matter for credibility. First, what a camera measures
is pulse rate variability (PRV), not heart rate variability; the two diverge
under conditions that affect pulse transit time (posture, cold hands, vascular
tone), and the difference is largest in exactly the short-term metrics this
library reports (Schäfer & Vagedes 2013; Mejía-Mejía et al. 2020). Name the
functions honestly (`prv.rmssd`) and explain the relationship once in the docs.
Second, every metric should carry its measurement floor for the current frame
rate and beat count, as computed in A3, so integrators can decide whether a
change is signal or noise.

## 6. Research-backed metrics worth adding, ranked by feasibility

Ranked by what a 30 to 60 fps fingertip camera can actually support. Items in
the first tier are worth doing now; the second tier is worth doing with a
"research" label; the third tier should be declined or fenced off no matter
how often users ask.

### Tier 1: feasible now, well supported

- **Ultra-short-term RMSSD (60 s) as the headline HRV number.** 60 s RMSSD at
  rest agrees closely with 5 min values (Esco & Flatt 2014), and a smartphone
  camera 1 min protocol has been validated against ECG and a chest strap at
  rest (Plews et al. 2017, HRV4Training vs Polar H7 vs ECG). This turns the
  3 min protocol into an optional long form and makes daily use realistic.
- **lnRMSSD with a personal baseline.** Report the natural log, keep a rolling
  7 day mean and coefficient of variation, and flag changes beyond the
  smallest worthwhile change (Plews et al. 2013). Pure functions; the app
  supplies storage. This is the feature people actually use HRV apps for.
- **Per-beat template-matching quality.** Correlate each detected pulse with
  the running average pulse template and reject beats below a threshold
  (Orphanidou et al. 2015). It rejects the *beat*, not the *interval*, which
  the current IBI-only rules cannot do, and it is cheap. Add the skewness
  signal-quality index as the per-window scalar (Elgendi 2016 found it the
  best single SQI for PPG).
- **Respiratory rate.** Extract respiratory-induced intensity, amplitude and
  frequency variation from the pulse train, estimate rate from each, and fuse
  with agreement-based quality (Karlen et al. 2013 "Smart Fusion"; Charlton
  et al. 2016 benchmark). The `respiration` event is already declared in the
  types; this makes it real. Report only when the three estimators agree.
- **Motion gate from `DeviceMotion`.** The README roadmap lists it; it is a
  few lines plus the iOS `requestPermission()` dance, and it lets the gate
  say "you moved" instead of "irregular beats".
- **Paced-breathing coherence.** For a guided session at about six breaths
  per minute, the ratio of peak power in 0.04 to 0.26 Hz to total power is
  the standard biofeedback score (Lehrer & Gevirtz 2014). Cheap once the
  frequency domain is fixed (C2, C3), and a natural demo screen.
- **pNN50, SD1/SD2, Poincaré plot.** Already in the demo; move into `/hrv`
  with tests and the corrected estimators (C6).
- **Measurement floor reporting** (A3): a metric, not a feature, but the one
  that makes the others honest.

### Tier 2: feasible with care, label as research

- **DFA α1.** In the demo already; it is used as a marker of aerobic threshold
  in exercise physiology (Rogers et al. 2021) and of autonomic balance at rest.
  Needs about 2 min of clean beats and is sensitive to artifacts, so gate it
  on the template SQI.
- **Sample entropy.** Keep, with the template-count fix (C6) and a minimum of
  100 to 200 beats.
- **Pulse-wave morphology.** Crest time, pulse width at half height, the
  stiffness index from the systolic to diastolic peak delay (Millasseau et al.
  2002), and the second-derivative "aging index" (Takazawa et al. 1998) are
  all published on fingertip PPG. They need the waveform at 100 Hz or better;
  with cubic interpolation from 60 fps the systolic-diastolic delay is
  marginal and the second derivative is noisy, so treat these as exploratory
  and validate against the literature's age curves before showing them.
- **Pulse amplitude variability and perfusion trend.** Beat-to-beat AC
  amplitude is a cheap vasomotion and respiration proxy and a good
  self-check for the AC/DC gate.
- **Rhythm irregularity index.** RMSSD normalised by mean RR, Shannon entropy
  of the RR histogram, and the turning-point ratio together separate
  irregular rhythms from sinus rhythm on smartphone PPG (Lee et al. 2013;
  McManus et al. 2013). Valuable and dangerous: any wording that implies
  atrial fibrillation detection is a regulated medical claim. Ship as an
  "irregularity score" for research with a disclaimer that it is not a
  screening tool.

### Tier 3: decline, or fence off very clearly

- **SpO2.** Single-camera smartphone oximetry has been shown to be feasible
  only with per-device calibration against induced hypoxaemia (Hoffman et al.
  2022). Without that calibration the number is a guess, and "SpO2" is a
  regulated claim.
- **Blood pressure from PPG morphology.** The evidence base is weak and
  calibration-dependent (Elgendi et al. 2019 review). Do not.
- **Population "stress" scores.** Until the ANS indices are built from cited
  reference distributions (C7), present relative change against the user's own
  baseline rather than z-scores against a population.
